# Technical merit, adoption, economics and vendor survival are different outcomes

Flux continued after Weaveworks closed; CDKTF was archived after its maintainer reported insufficient product-market fit. Those outcomes answer different questions. The first separates a project from a company; the second limits the success of one product rather than every language-based infrastructure approach.[^flux][^cdktf]

Use four independent questions. Does the mechanism work? Who uses it, in which population? Does it improve the full operating economics? Who funds and maintains the implementation? Evidence for one answer should not silently supply the other three.

Acquisitions are especially ambiguous. Fermyon’s acquisition establishes a transaction and a stated continuation path, but not a broad verdict that WebAssembly won or failed.[^wasm]

For a platform dependency, include a maintainer and migration assessment alongside feature evaluation. For an investment thesis, ask whether user value can support a durable business without assuming that a popular open-source project automatically produces a viable vendor.
