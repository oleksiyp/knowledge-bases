# Summary
The Common Good Cyber Fund (CGCF) pools money from the governments of Australia, Canada, Germany, the Netherlands and the UK, plus Craig Newmark Philanthropies and the Internet Society.[^cgcf-page] It funds nonprofits that do cybersecurity work for the common good, including protecting journalists and civil society.[^cgcf-2026-pr] After six pilot grants in 2025, the 2026 call (23 June – 4 August 2026) planned at least $3.5M across about 15 two-year grants of typically $100k–$300k.[^cgcf-page][^cgcf-2026-pr] It sits inside ISOC's Safer Internet Initiative, which plans more than $40M for 2026–2029.[^cgcf-2026-pr]

# Eligibility
- 501(c)(3) nonprofits or international equivalents focused on cybersecurity that can receive funds from the US.[^cgcf-page] Applications can be in English, French or Spanish.[^cgcf-2026-pr]

# What it funds
1. Maintaining critical infrastructure, e.g. DNS security, threat intelligence and open systems upkeep.[^cgcf-page]
2. Scalable protection from digital harm, e.g. incident response, account recovery and help for high-risk users facing state-directed threats.[^cgcf-page]
3. A safer Internet for vulnerable groups, through policy and coordination that benefits journalists and civil society.[^cgcf-page]

# Amounts & terms
- $100k–$300k over two years.[^cgcf-page]

- **Good fit if:** you are a nonprofit running security infrastructure (DNS, CERT-style services, helplines) or open security tooling for civil society.
- **Poor fit if:** you are a company or an individual.
