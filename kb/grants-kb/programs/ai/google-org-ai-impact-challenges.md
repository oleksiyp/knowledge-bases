# Summary

Google.org runs large open calls that pair cash grants with a technical accelerator: Google engineers, mentors, and Cloud credits. In 2026 it ran two **$30M** challenges. **AI for Science** paid $500k–$3M per organization across health and life sciences, crisis resilience and environmental science, with a 17 April 2026 deadline.[^gorg-science] **AI for Government Innovation** paid $1M–$3M to nonprofits, social enterprises and academics partnering with governments, with a 3 April 2026 deadline.[^gorg-gov][^gorg-gov-opdesk] Under the earlier Generative AI Accelerator rules, IP created with Google.org funding must be released under a permissive open-source license.[^gorg-genai-faq] No new call is open as of October 2026.

# Eligibility

- Nonprofits, civic entities, academic institutions and social enterprises.[^gorg-genai-faq][^gorg-gov-opdesk]
- AI for Government applicants needed a government partner.[^gorg-gov-opdesk]

# What it funds

- Building AI-powered social-impact solutions, for example scientific discovery tools or public-service agents.[^gorg-science][^gorg-gov]

# Amounts & terms

- See the ranges above. Funded IP must be openly licensed (permissive OSS).[^gorg-genai-faq]

# How to apply

- Online application with evaluation on impact, innovative use of technology, feasibility and scalability.[^gorg-gov-opdesk]

- The Generative AI Accelerator's second cohort (2025) had 20 organizations working on crisis response, antimicrobial resistance and youth mental health.[^gorg-genai-2025]

# Fit

- **Good fit if:** you are a nonprofit or academic team with a working AI prototype and a scaling plan.
- **Poor fit if:** you are a for-profit startup or an individual developer.
