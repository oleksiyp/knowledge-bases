# Summary

FUTO funds software that gives users control of their computing, in two tiers.[^futo-grants] "Legendary" grants are large, ongoing awards to established projects and nonprofits: more than $5M across 20 projects, including Signal, Tor, Ladybird and CalyxOS. Microgrants are one-time awards of $1,000–$5,000, with more than $60,000 given to 52 projects, including Blender, GIMP, VLC, KeePassXC, Lichess, Neovim, Svelte, Tauri and Typst.[^futo-grants] Applications are open year-round by email.[^futo-grants] The listed microgrants run from May 2023 to August 2025.[^futo-grants]

# Eligibility

- Open-source projects and their maintainers (individuals included), plus nonprofits.[^futo-grants]

# What it funds

- Microgrants are thank-you or boost grants for useful open-source software. Legendary grants provide sustained support for major freedom-respecting projects.[^futo-grants]

# Amounts & terms

- Microgrants are $1,000–$5,000 and one-time. Legendary grant sizes are not published per project, but exceed $5M in total.[^futo-grants]

# How to apply

- Email grantapps@futo.org. FUTO notes that many microgrants are unsolicited, often recommended by its employees.[^futo-grants]

# Deadlines

Rolling.

# Track record

- More than $5M in legendary grants (20 projects) and more than $60K in microgrants (52 projects).[^futo-grants]

# Fit

- **Good fit if** you maintain a popular open-source tool that gives users autonomy, and want a small no-strings boost.
- **Poor fit if** you need salary-level funding without an existing track record.
