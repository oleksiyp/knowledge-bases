# Summary

1517 Fund's Medici Project gives young builders a minimum $1,000 stipend, paid via Venmo with no strings attached. The fund takes no ownership of the recipient, the project or its IP.[^medici] It targets high-school and college students, dropouts and deep-tech scientists working on passion projects outside school credit.[^medici] Grants are limited to North America.[^medici] In 2024 1517 said it "strongly prefer[s] HW over SW" for these grants, so software-only projects are a weaker fit.[^medici-x]

# Eligibility

- High-school students, college students, dropouts and deep-tech scientists.[^medici]
- Teams must be based in North America because of regulatory limits, though others may still inquire.[^medici]
- Projects must not be for grades or admissions.[^medici]

# What it funds

- Materials, prototypes, server costs and travel for early, curiosity-driven projects in science, tech, maths or art.[^medici]

# Amounts & terms

- A minimum of $1,000, with no equity or IP claims.[^medici]

# How to apply

- Email a team member (firstname@1517fund.com) with a five-minute Loom video covering who you are, why the project matters to you, and how the grant helps. Expect a reply within about two weeks if the project fits the fund's focus.[^medici]

Rolling.

# Fit

- **Good fit if** you are a North American student or dropout with a scrappy hardware or software prototype.
- **Poor fit if** you are outside North America, or need more than a few thousand dollars.
