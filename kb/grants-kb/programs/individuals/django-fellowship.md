# Summary

The Django Software Foundation pays Fellows as contractors to keep Django healthy: security monitoring, releases, ticket triage, pull request review and supporting contributors.[^dsf-fellow-call-2025] It is a long-running example of a foundation paying individual maintainers, and the program has existed since 2014.[^dsf-fellow-call-2025] In 2026 there are three Fellows (Natalia Bidart part-time, Sarah Boyce and Jacob Walls). The Fellowship is the DSF's largest expense.[^dsf-fundraising] New positions open only when fundraising allows. The most recent call closed on 1 July 2025.[^dsf-fellow-call-2025]

# Eligibility

- Senior generalist engineers with extensive Python and Django experience. Being a Django contributor already is not required.[^dsf-fellow-call-2025]

# What it funds

- Maintenance work on Django itself (not your own project).[^dsf-fellow-call-2025]

# Amounts & terms

- $82.26/hour (2025 rate) on a one-year contract, renewable depending on fundraising.[^dsf-fellow-call-2025]

# How to apply

- When a call is open, email fellowship-committee@djangoproject.com with your experience, community involvement and at least one recommendation.[^dsf-fellow-call-2025]

# Track record

- Fellows triage 10–15 new tickets a week and review or merge around 15 non-trivial patches a week.[^dsf-fundraising]
- The DSF was 47% of the way to its $500,000 2026 fundraising goal when checked.[^dsf-fundraising]

# Fit

- **Good fit if** you are a seasoned Django developer who wants paid maintainer work.
- **Poor fit if** you need funding for your own project.
