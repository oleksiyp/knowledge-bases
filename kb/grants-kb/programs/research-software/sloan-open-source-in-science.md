# Summary

Open Source in Science is the current Sloan Technology-program line for research software. It replaces the earlier **Better Software for Science** line, which Sloan now marks as no longer accepting proposals.[^sloan-bss][^sloan-ossci] It funds the **tools, norms and institutions** that let open-source practice support distributed development, adoption and maintenance of discovery-enabling software, hardware and models. Sloan says it mostly does **not** fund development of individual open-source projects.[^sloan-ossci] Current focus areas are roles and career paths, institutional support for open source (for example university OSPOs), and publication and archiving.[^okfn-sloan] Letters of inquiry are accepted on a rolling basis.[^sloan-ossci]

# Eligibility

- Mostly universities, research institutions and non-profits. Sloan does not fund individuals directly (check sloan.org/grants/apply).[^okfn-sloan]

# What it funds

- Tooling, institutions, economic models and incentives around producing, maintaining and adopting research software, hardware and models.[^sloan-ossci]
- Examples from 2026: AI-assisted refactoring and testing of legacy research code (University of North Florida, September 2026), a Research Software Support Network run by the Johns Hopkins OSPO, and continued support for the University of California OSPO network.[^unf-sloan][^jhu-ospo-sloan][^ucsb-ospo-sloan]
- Not funded: feature work on a single project.[^sloan-ossci]

# Amounts & terms

- Not published for this line. Sloan Technology exploratory grants listed by third parties range from about $100K to $400K (unverified for Open Source in Science specifically).[^granted-sloan-egt]

# How to apply

- Send a short letter of inquiry through Sloan's portal. If invited, submit a full proposal with a program-officer dialogue.[^okfn-sloan]

# Deadlines

Rolling; no fixed deadline.[^sloan-ossci]

# Track record

- See 2026 examples above. Sloan also funds university OSPO networks, and was an early funder of pyOpenSci.[^ucsb-ospo-sloan][^pyos-czi]

# Fit

- **Good fit if:** you are building institutional capacity for research software, such as an OSPO, RSE career paths, software citation or archiving infrastructure.
- **Poor fit if:** you want maintenance money for your own library (look at OS4Science, CZI-successor funds, NumFOCUS SDG).
