# Summary

The NIH Office of Data Science Strategy (ODSS) has funded biomedical research software sustainability in two ways. First, administrative supplements to existing NIH grants ("Enhancement of Software Tools for Open Science").[^odss-supplements] Second, since 2024, a standalone R03 award, *Building Sustainable Software Tools for Open Science*, which pays for collaborations between scientists and software engineers to make tools robust, scalable, reproducible and widely disseminated.[^nih-rfa-od-24-010] The 2024 RFA (RFA-OD-24-010) had deadlines in December 2024, June 2025 and December 2025.[^niso-od-24-010] A reissue, **RFA-OD-26-034**, was forecast on February 5, 2026, with an estimated due date of June 16, 2026. As of October 2026, public listings still show it as *forecasted*, so treat the next cycle as **unconfirmed**.[^sg-rfa-od-26-034][^highergov-od-26-034]

# Eligibility

- Broad: U.S. higher education, non-profits, for-profits and small businesses, governments, Tribal organizations, and **non-U.S. entities**, according to the forecast listing.[^sg-rfa-od-26-034]
- Clinical trials not allowed (R03).[^sg-rfa-od-26-034]

# What it funds

- Best-practice engineering of existing biomedical, clinical, behavioral or health research software: robustness, sustainability, scalability, reproducibility, dissemination, and collaboration between scientists and software engineers, as well as workforce software skills.[^sg-rfa-od-26-034]

# Amounts & terms

- Prior cycles were reported at up to $300,000 direct costs for up to two years. The 2026 ceiling has not been published (unverified).[^highergov-od-26-034]

# How to apply

- Watch Grants.gov/NIH Guide for RFA-OD-26-034 posting. Questions: sustainablesoftware@nih.gov.[^sg-rfa-od-26-034]
