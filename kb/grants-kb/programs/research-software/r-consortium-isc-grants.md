# Summary

The R Consortium's ISC funds projects with **broad impact on the R community**. These can be technical infrastructure (packages, database tools, R platform improvements) or social infrastructure (community coordination). Proposals need a focused scope and clear deliverables.[^rc-cfp] There are two cycles a year. In 2026 they ran **April 1 – May 1** (awards announced July 14, 2026: 7 grants, $40,500 total) and **September 1 – October 1**, with notifications on November 1, 2026.[^rc-cfp][^rc-2026-r1]

# Eligibility

- Anyone with a project of broad benefit to R users. Individuals and teams have been funded.[^rc-2026-r1]

# What it funds

- Package maintenance and modernization, interoperability, domain toolkits, and community resources.[^rc-cfp]
- Not funded: projects for small user groups, conference sponsorship (use the RUGS program instead), exploratory research, or hardware.[^rc-cfp]

# Amounts & terms

- 2026 round 1 awards ranged from $3,700 to $10,000.[^rc-2026-r1]
- **50% paid at contract signing, 50% on completion.**[^rc-cfp]

# How to apply

- A 2–5 page proposal on the required template, as a self-contained PDF, submitted through a Google Form. The ISC chair assigns reviewers.[^rc-cfp]

# Track record

- 2026-1: tigris/tidycensus maintenance ($7,000), sfnetworks 1.0 ($10,000), igvShiny ($4,000), partitions ($4,200), redlist ($6,600), WeedR ($3,700), ALTREP strings ($5,000).[^rc-2026-r1]

# Fit

- **Good fit if:** you maintain an R package or tool used well beyond your own lab and have a clear, scoped deliverable.
- **Poor fit if:** you need salary-scale money.
