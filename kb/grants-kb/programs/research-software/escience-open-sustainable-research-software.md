# Summary

In January 2026 Open Science NL awarded the Netherlands eScience Center **€5.4M** for a Research Software Sustainability Programme. It has two pillars: sustainability calls and a fellowship programme.[^osnl-esc] The first call, **OSS 2026**, funds **8 projects** (2 per research area). Each gets about **€225,000 of in-kind RSE work** (roughly 2,000 hours) plus up to **€25,000** for workshops, training and community activity, over at most 2 years.[^esc-oss-2026] Project proposals closed May 14, 2026 and full proposals September 3, 2026. Decisions are due in December 2026.[^esc-oss-2026]

# Eligibility

- Applicants at eligible Dutch research organizations, including universities of applied sciences (hogescholen).[^esc-oss-2026]
- The software must already exist, be publicly available in version control, and show adoption through at least five publications or other public research outputs.[^esc-oss-2026]
- Teams must span institutions and include community representatives.[^esc-oss-2026]

# What it funds

- Wider disciplinary applicability, better software quality and maturity, and research communities around the software.[^esc-oss-2026]
- New software development is **not eligible**.[^esc-oss-2026]

# Amounts & terms

- Mostly in-kind (eScience Center RSEs work on your code), plus a small cash budget for activities.[^esc-oss-2026]

# How to apply

- Two stages: a short project proposal, then a full proposal, with proposal-development workshops in between (July 6 and August 17, 2026).[^esc-oss-2026]

# Track record

- OSS 2026 is the first call under the Open Science NL-funded programme. Earlier eScience Center "Call for Sustainable Software" rounds ran in previous years.[^esc-calls]

# Fit

- **Good fit if:** you are a Dutch team with adopted research software that needs engineering muscle.
- **Poor fit if:** you want cash for your own developers, or you are not in the Netherlands.
