# Summary

ITCR is NCI's long-running program for cancer-research informatics software. It funds a tool at each stage: **early-stage development** (U01), **advanced development and dissemination** of emerging tools (U24), and **sustainment** of established, widely adopted tools (U24).[^rfa-ca-27-019][^rfa-ca-27-020][^rfa-ca-27-021] The FY2027 notices (RFA-CA-27-019/020/021) were posted May 21, 2026. All three share an **October 19, 2026** due date, and the U01 continues to June 11, 2027 and October 19, 2027.[^rfa-ca-27-019][^rfa-ca-27-021] Award numbers are small: about 5 U01s (≈$2.4M), about 3 advanced U24s (≈$2.6M) and 2 sustainment U24s (≈$1.8M) in FY2027.[^rfa-ca-27-019][^rfa-ca-27-020][^rfa-ca-27-021]

# Eligibility

- Higher education, non-profits, for-profits, governments and others. The sustainment NOFO lists **foreign organizations as eligible**, but NIH no longer funds foreign subawards or subcontracts unless the NOFO specifically allows them.[^rfa-ca-27-021]
- The sustainment track requires evidence that the tool is established and has significant impact on cancer research.[^rfa-ca-27-021]
- The advanced-development track is for tools past prototyping that are not yet widely adopted.[^rfa-ca-27-020]

# What it funds

- Informatics technologies for acquiring, managing, analyzing and disseminating data across the cancer research continuum, from basic biology to epidemiology and clinical care.[^rfa-ca-27-021]
- Sustainment covers operations, maintenance and keeping a widely used tool scientifically relevant.[^rfa-ca-27-021]

# Fit

- **Good fit if:** your tool is used in cancer research and you can show adoption (sustainment track) or a working prototype (advanced track).
- **Poor fit if:** the tool is not cancer-relevant, or the work depends on foreign subcontracts.
