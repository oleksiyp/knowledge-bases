# Summary

IDSS (NSF 26-509) is the Office of Advanced Cyberinfrastructure's 2026 solicitation for data systems and services that serve research communities. It has three categories: large national systems, mid-scale systems, and small pilots.[^nsf-26-509] It is not a pure software program, but every IDSS project must release the source code of any software it develops under an identified open-source license, so it is a large source of research software engineering money.[^nsf-26-509] NSF expects 3–9 awards per cycle. The Category II deadline passed on July 28, 2026, and the next deadline (Categories I and III) is **July 27, 2027**.[^nsf-26-509]

# Eligibility

- U.S. institutions of higher education, non-profit non-academic organizations (museums, research labs and similar), and other federal agencies and FFRDCs.[^nsf-26-509]
- An organization may submit only one proposal as lead institution in each of Category I and Category II. A PI or co-PI may be on only one Category I–II proposal per deadline.[^nsf-26-509]

# What it funds

- Category I: national-scale integrated data systems and services ($10–30M, up to 5 years, 1–2 awards).[^nsf-26-509]
- Category II: mid-scale systems (up to $9M, up to 3 years, 1–2 awards).[^nsf-26-509]
- Category III: pilots and planning (up to $500K, up to 2 years, 1–5 awards, not renewable).[^nsf-26-509]

# Amounts & terms

See above. Categories I and II may be renewed. All software must be open source with a named license.[^nsf-26-509]

# How to apply

Full proposals go through Research.gov. Large categories usually need institutional commitment, so plan any internal limited-submission process early.[^nsf-26-509]

# Fit

- **Good fit if:** you run, or want to pilot, a community data platform with significant software engineering at a U.S. university or non-profit lab.
