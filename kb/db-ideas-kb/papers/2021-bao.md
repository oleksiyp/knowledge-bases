# Claim
Bao steers an existing optimizer rather than generating every plan from scratch. It combines representations of candidate plans with Thompson sampling to select per-query optimization hints, then learns from execution feedback. The authors target the training overhead, poor adaptation and tail-latency problems of earlier learned optimizers.[^bao-preprint] The final paper appeared at SIGMOD 2021 and received a best-paper award.[^bao-final]

# What happened next
Microsoft's later QO-Advisor production account describes the related steering approach with offline exploration, cost budgets and regression safeguards, enabled by default for SCOPE workloads.[^qo-advisor] That is evidence for the practical direction; it should not be described as proof that Microsoft deployed the Bao prototype itself.

Our assessment is that Bao made the boundary between a learned component and a mature optimizer more useful. A bounded choice among hints preserves much of the engine's accumulated planning knowledge and reduces the work needed to integrate learning. It does not remove exploration risk: a legal hint can still produce a slow plan, and changing workloads can invalidate past experience. The important transferable result is to constrain the action space and observe outcomes, while distinguishing research speedups from the operational work needed for deployment.
