# Summary
From 2018 to 2026 S3 changed from "where backups and data lakes live" into the **substrate of new databases**: Snowflake and lakehouse formats, Neon's page layer, WarpStream and diskless Kafka, turbopuffer and SlateDB. AWS added the primitives those systems needed:
- **S3 Express One Zone** (Nov 2023): single-AZ, low-latency tier. Prices cut by up to 85% on GETs in Apr 2025[^s3x-cut].
- **Conditional writes**: `If-None-Match` create-if-absent (Aug 20, 2024)[^s3-cond], then `If-Match` compare-and-swap (Nov 2024)[^s3-ifmatch]. Leader election, manifest commits and writer fencing became possible without DynamoDB or ZooKeeper.
- **S3 Tables** (Dec 2024): managed Iceberg tables. More than 2M tables by 2026[^vogels-s3files].
- **S3 Vectors**: preview Jul 2025, GA Dec 2, 2025 with up to 2B vectors per index, claimed up to 90% cheaper than vector databases[^s3v-preview][^s3v-ga].
- **S3 Files** (Apr 2026): file-system access to S3 data[^vogels-s3files].

# What worked
- Durability and cost economics that databases now build on rather than compete with.
- Adding one primitive (CAS) unlocked many coordinator-free designs.

# What didn't / limits
- Standard-tier latency (tens of ms) and per-request pricing still force batching and caching.
- Express One Zone gives up multi-AZ durability for speed.
- At petabyte scale with steady load, S3 can be beaten on cost by owned flash: 37signals replaced a ~$1.5M/yr S3 bill with on-prem storage under $200k/yr[^stack-s3exit].
