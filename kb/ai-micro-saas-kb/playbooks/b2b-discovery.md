# B2B discovery before broad promotion

The [Lumoo case](/cases/lumoo.md) motivates examining domain knowledge, while its missing acquisition data prevents claiming a proven sales formula. The following is an original experiment design.

## Select a reachable workflow

Name a specific operator, the task they repeat, its current workaround, and the person who can authorize payment. Avoid starting with “AI for small businesses”: that describes neither a task nor a buying process. Ask for examples from the last occurrence, not opinions about whether the idea sounds useful.

Suggested interview prompts:

- What happened the last time you did this task?
- What input and finished output did you use?
- Where did work stop, require rework, or wait on someone?
- What do you currently pay in money or staff time?
- Who would approve a trial, and what would stop deployment?

These are learning prompts, not a script for unsolicited mass messaging.

## Offer a bounded paid pilot

Define one input, one delivered outcome, the acceptance criteria, the duration, the owner, and the price. State which work is assisted manually. For an early product, transparent manual operations can test value without pretending automation is complete.

An agency, association, or existing service provider may introduce a suitable pilot customer. Record that as a source. A warm introduction and cold outreach are different acquisition conditions.

## Decide from behavior

An enthusiastic interview is weaker evidence than access to real sample work; sample work is weaker than a paid pilot; a paid pilot is weaker than continued use under normal terms. Record objections even when they imply the product should not be built.

A proposed two-week test can target a small set of qualified conversations rather than a fixed revenue goal. If access is repeatedly blocked, first test a different reachable segment or trusted intermediary. Do not assume more automated outreach will fix an unclear offer. See [partnerships](/channels/partnerships.md).
