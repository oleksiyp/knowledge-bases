# A 30-day distribution experiment for a small AI-assisted product

This plan is a proposal. Adjust the schedule for sales cycle and task cadence. It deliberately stops short of claiming retention within thirty days when the product’s natural renewal interval is longer.

| Period | Work | Deliverable | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Days 1–5 | Observe a reachable buyer’s recurring task and current workaround | One segment, one promise, recorded objections | Is there an urgent job and a way to reach the buyer? |
| Days 6–10 | Build or adapt the smallest usable workflow and payment path | Honest demo; predefined activation event | Can a real user complete the promised task? |
| Days 11–17 | Run one chosen channel test with a fixed cap on money and time | Source-tagged visits, conversations, or pilot starts | Is the audience qualified, and where does it drop off? |
| Days 18–24 | Fix the dominant obstacle; repeat the same test | Comparable second cohort, documented change | Did useful activation or paid use improve? |
| Days 25–30 | Reconcile receipts, refunds, delivery costs, and available repeat use | Small cohort report and next decision | Continue, change the offer, change the channel, or stop? |

## Before spending

Write down the hypothesis, the budget you can lose, the buyer group, the conversion event, and the reason you would stop. Do not set a generic target borrowed from a successful founder. A €20 one-off task and a €500 monthly workflow cannot use the same acceptable acquisition cost.

## After the test

Keep both successful and unsuccessful creatives, dates, and denominators. Ask paying and non-paying users what happened. A small sample may only justify another small test. When a promising channel emerges, document the repeatable work and the parts that depended on luck or relationships.

Use [channel selection](/channel-selection.md), [demo design](/playbooks/attention-to-activation.md), and [measurement](/playbooks/measurement.md) together. The goal of the month is an honest decision backed by observed behavior.
