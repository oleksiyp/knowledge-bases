# AI micro-SaaS: what builders make and how customers find it

## What the evidence supports

AI-assisted development lowers the effort needed to put a product in front of people; the cases do not show that it removes the work of acquiring customers. The useful distinction is between **building a product**, **getting attention**, **activating a buyer**, and **retaining profitable use**. No public case here supplies independently audited data for the entire chain.

This bundle contains 13 case studies covering 14 named products, 11 channel reviews, and practical experiment guides. Six case studies explicitly report AI-assisted implementation. The remaining seven are AI-powered comparators, including one paired failure study. Transactional services, physical commerce, funded companies, and businesses that grew beyond micro scale are labeled rather than silently treated as recurring solo SaaS.

## What people are building

| Product family | Examples | Buyer’s job | Distribution question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mundane task automation | [PrintPigeon](/cases/printpigeon.md), [BuzzerBee](/cases/buzzerbee.md) | Complete a specific inconvenient task | Where does the need become visible? |
| Family and consumer outputs | [Spellbound](/cases/spellbound.md), [Frame My Home](/cases/frame-my-home.md) | Practice or obtain a personalized result | Who already reaches the relevant household? |
| Vertical business workflows | [Lumoo](/cases/lumoo.md), [Crown Services](/cases/crown-services.md) | Run a specialized operation | Who owns the budget and trusts a new supplier? |
| AI productivity interfaces | [TypingMind](/cases/typingmind.md), [PDF.ai](/cases/pdf-ai.md), [Jenni](/cases/jenni.md) | Improve an existing document or writing workflow | Can a demonstration lead directly to useful work? |
| Business chat and support | [Chatbase](/cases/chatbase.md), [SiteGPT](/cases/sitegpt.md) | Answer questions using business content | Does the buyer reach a successful setup? |
| Professional image services | [HeadshotPro](/cases/headshotpro.md) | Obtain usable professional images | Can reviewers demonstrate believable results? |

The linked case pages provide dated evidence and source attribution. These categories are an editorial grouping, not a market-size estimate.

## How attention becomes distribution — synthesis

1. **A recognizable result makes attention useful.** Design the demonstration around the buyer’s task rather than the development tool. A founder-story audience and a customer audience are not interchangeable.
2. **Existing access changes the experiment.** Separate a launch backed by a following, a partner relationship, or industry credibility from a true cold start. The [comparison](/evidence-matrix.md) makes those differences visible.
3. **A burst and a repeatable channel are different outcomes.** A launch can seed a cohort; a useful tool, targeted content, creator relationship, or partner placement can provide a reason for discovery afterward. Repetition alone does not establish profitability.
4. **A small paid result is informative.** A modest business with explicit missing evidence is more useful than an impressive headline with unclear units. Compare recurring receipts with recurring costs and track cohorts.
5. **The negative cases constrain the advice.** [Failed products](/cases/emojiai-askcommand.md), unresolved distribution, missing attribution, and high-reach low-conversion examples prevent a universal “just ship and post” conclusion.

## Where to begin — recommendation, not a ranking

For a founder with no audience, start with a buyer group they can actually reach and a task they can observe. Use [B2B discovery](/playbooks/b2b-discovery.md) for specialist workflows, [search](/channels/search.md) for explicit existing demand, and [creator experiments](/channels/creators.md) for demonstrable consumer outcomes. If there is no plausible route to a buyer, test access before expanding the product.
