# Paid search: buy a small demand test before buying scale

## Evidence

[PrintPigeon](/cases/printpigeon.md) reports a small Google Ads trial followed by a change in emphasis because ads alone were not sustainable.[^printpigeon] This is a useful limit on the claim that an AI-built product becomes easy to market.

## Mechanism and buyer fit — analysis

Paid search makes it possible to test a specific promise against a specific task. It does not ensure demand, acceptable margins, or repeat use. A low-priced one-off service and a high-retention subscription have different allowable acquisition costs.

## A testable experiment — proposal

Choose one tightly defined job, a matching landing page, a spend cap, and a paid-completion event. Inspect actual queries and failed checkout attempts. Stop or revise when the test cannot plausibly reach contribution-margin payback. Only extend the budget after enough observations to distinguish a pattern from a lucky sale.

## Limits

No universal budget, click price, or sample size follows from this sample. Include campaign setup time and refunds; do not optimize only for cheap signups. Paid social is a separate hypothesis, not validated by this search example.

Use the common [measurement definitions](/playbooks/measurement.md); these proposals are not industry benchmarks.
