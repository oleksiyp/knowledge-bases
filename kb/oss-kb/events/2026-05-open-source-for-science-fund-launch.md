# What happened
Renaissance Philanthropy launched the Open Source for Science Fund on 2026-05-04, "seeded with $20 million in anchor funding by Biohub and Wellcome and support from The Kavli Foundation and the Research Software Alliance"[^os4s-launch]. It is directed by Dario Taraborelli and was "built and designed by the team behind EOSS", the CZI program that deployed $58M across 230+ projects over six cycles[^rp-launch]. The first call, *Open Source for the Life Sciences*, offers Track 1 grants up to $250k (domain tools) and Track 2 grants up to $1M (foundational libraries and ecosystem initiatives, with attention to "AI readiness"), both over two years; LOIs were due 2026-06-08, full proposals 2026-07-21, decisions expected October 2026[^os4ls].

# Why it matters
CZI's EOSS — the largest dedicated scientific-OSS funder — had issued no new call since its sixth RFA closed in October 2023[^czi-eoss-rfa], and 2025 brought deep disruption to US federal science grants. The new fund restores a multi-funder, maintainer-led grant line, but with a smaller seed and a life-sciences/AI framing rather than EOSS's broad remit[^rp-launch][^ssi-os4s].

# Outcome so far
Hundreds of teams submitted LOIs to the inaugural call; first awards are due in October 2026 (not yet announced as of 2026-10-03)[^os4ls].
