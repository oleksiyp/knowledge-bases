# What happened
On Sept 9, 2026 the board voted to put Mullenweg on paid leave and installed CFO Mark Davies as interim CEO.[^tc-leave] Mullenweg regained control after about 33 hours. Automattic confirmed on Sept 12 that he is "chairman and CEO … with full support of the board".[^tc-return] The interim CEO and the chief legal officer had signed reciprocal severance deals during the leave, which are now under review.[^tc-severance] A new board was formed by Sept 25. Departures include Toni Schneider, Sue Decker (resigned) and Ann Dunwoody (removed), and the new members include Hugh Howey, Amy Chan and two former executives of the app IRL. Davies and general counsel Andy Missan were asked to step down.[^tc-newboard][^techzine-board] No reason for the board's action was disclosed.[^techzine-board]

# Why it matters
The steward of the WordPress trademark and WordPress.org infrastructure is now governed by a board hand-picked by its founder, two years into litigation over his use of that control.

# Outcome so far
In a ruling dated Sept 24, 2026 the court revived WP Engine's antitrust claims and held that Automattic does not own the WordPress marks, so its trademark claims cannot proceed; trial is set for Oct 19, 2027 (see [ruling event](/events/2026-09-wp-engine-antitrust-revived-trademark-ruling.md) and the [lawsuit event](/events/2024-10-wp-engine-sues-automattic.md)).
