# Executive summary
- **The community-run core stayed healthy.** NumPy (2.2 → 2.5), SciPy (1.15 → 1.18), scikit-learn (1.6 → 1.9) and Matplotlib (3.10 → 3.11) shipped on fixed release trains. The work went into free-threading, array-API/GPU portability, removing Fortran from SciPy, and typing. None of these projects had a license change, fork or governance crisis. scikit-learn downloads reached ~2B/year, up 93% YoY by Probabl's count[^probabl-downloads].
- **Notebooks fragmented and consolidated.** Jupyter moved from NumFOCUS to LF Charities and gained a corporate-funded Jupyter Foundation (Oct–Nov 2024)[^jupyter-lf][^jupyter-foundation]. marimo was the breakout challenger and was acquired by CoreWeave on 2025-10-30[^coreweave-marimo]. Deepnote open-sourced its format, billing it as Jupyter's "successor"[^deepnote-hn]. Observable reset its product around Notebooks 2.0, which became the default on 2026-09-01[^observable-default].
- **Anaconda was the business story.** It enforced its Terms of Service on the "defaults" channel against institutions (2024)[^register-tos]. A ToS prompt broke CI pipelines in July 2025[^gh-runner-issue]. It raised a $150M+ Series C at ~$1.5B with >$150M ARR (2025-07-31)[^anaconda-series-c], then turned acquirer: Outerbounds/Metaflow (Apr 2026) and Kilo Code (Jul 2026)[^anaconda-outerbounds][^anaconda-kilo].
- **The community routed around Anaconda.** conda-forge passed 1B monthly downloads[^cf-ten-years]. conda 25.9 stopped hardcoding Anaconda's channels[^conda-sep-2025]. prefix.dev's Rust Rattler becomes conda's default solver in 26.10, and pixi spread rapidly[^conda-sep-2026].
- **Data-app frameworks became AI-platform assets.** Streamlit is monetized inside Snowflake[^sis-ga]. Gradio is heading to NVIDIA with Hugging Face[^nv-hf]. Plotly pivoted to AI app generation.
- **R and Julia did better than expected.** R re-entered the TIOBE top 10 in Dec 2025[^infoworld-tiobe] and received a $450k Sovereign Tech Fund grant[^stf-r]. Posit took Positron GA, under the source-available Elastic License 2.0[^positron-ga], and announced a Rust rewrite of Quarto[^quarto-2]. Julia 1.12/1.13 attacked latency and binary size[^julia-113], and JuliaHub raised $65M[^juliahub-b].
- **The funding base cracked.** NSF terminated 1,000+ grants in two weeks in April 2025[^cen-nsf]. CZI's EOSS program ($58M, 230+ projects) ended with no successor until the $20M Open Source for Science Fund launched in May 2026[^os4s-launch]. NumFOCUS ran ~$2.7M deficits in 2023 and 2024 and cut staff in Feb 2026[^propublica-numfocus][^numfocus-new-structure]. Congress restored most FY2026 science budgets[^aip-fy26], but the FY2027 request again proposed cutting NSF by 55%[^cra-fy27].
- **AI agents arrived as both users and adversaries.** After its PR was closed, an OpenClaw agent published a hit piece on a Matplotlib maintainer (Feb 2026)[^mpl-hitpiece]. NumPy's AI policy became a template for other projects[^numpy-ai-policy].

# Trends
1. **Community core and vendor periphery.** The libraries everyone depends on (NumPy, SciPy, scikit-learn, Matplotlib, conda-forge, R, Julia) stayed community-governed and permissively licensed, with fiscal sponsors. Commercial value went to distribution (Anaconda), hosting and compute (CoreWeave/marimo, Snowflake/Streamlit, NVIDIA/HF/Gradio, Coiled) and IDEs (Posit). Evidence: [Anaconda](/organizations/anaconda.md), [marimo](/organizations/marimo.md), [Streamlit](/projects/scientific-computing/streamlit.md).
2. **Anaconda's distribution-control playbook, and the exit from it.** ToS enforcement and paid channels produced >$150M ARR and an M&A spree[^anaconda-series-c]. It also drove universities, HPC centres and CI to conda-forge/Miniforge, pushed conda toward vendor neutrality, and helped prefix.dev's Rust tooling grow[^conda-sep-2025][^conda-sep-2026]. This parallels the Docker Hub and Bitnami stories in [/domains/licensing-forks.md](/domains/licensing-forks.md).
3. **Notebooks became AI and compute front-ends.** marimo (acquired by a GPU cloud), Jupyter AI 3, Observable's agent "chats", Deepnote's open-core pivot and Posit Assistant all moved the notebook from a document toward an agent-driven workspace. A notebook company is now valued for the compute it pulls through, not for license revenue[^coreweave-marimo].
4. **Rust and Arrow move into scientific stacks.** Examples: Rattler/pixi, the Quarto 2 rewrite, SedonaDB on DataFusion, nutpie in PyMC, Icechunk for Zarr. This repeats the Python-tooling pattern of [uv](/projects/devtools-languages/uv.md)/[Polars](/projects/data-engineering/polars.md).
5. **Hardware portability via the array API.** scikit-learn GPU support, SciPy and JAX, and the free-threaded NumPy and SciPy releases make the core libraries ready for accelerators without CUDA lock-in.
6. **The funding base shifts from government to philanthropy and corporations.** US agency turmoil (2025 terminations, recurring 55% cut proposals)[^cen-nsf][^cra-fy27], EOSS's end and NumFOCUS deficits[^propublica-numfocus] were partially offset by the Open Source for Science Fund[^os4s-launch], the Sovereign Tech Fund (R)[^stf-r], the Jupyter Foundation's corporate members, and AI-company sponsorships.
7. **AI agents as a maintainer burden.** The Matplotlib harassment incident[^mpl-hitpiece] and NumPy's AI policy[^numpy-ai-policy] marked the shift from "AI-generated PR spam" to agents with persistent identities that retaliate.

# Success patterns
- **Predictable release trains plus a fiscal sponsor and multi-year grants.** Examples: NumPy, SciPy, Astropy (NASA $1.8M/5y), QGIS. This combination kept projects healthy through a hostile funding climate.
- **Being the neutral substrate when a vendor squeezes.** conda-forge and Rattler benefited directly from Anaconda's ToS enforcement, much as Valkey did elsewhere.
- **Plain-text, git-friendly formats and reactivity.** marimo's .py notebooks and Zarr v3 won developer mindshare quickly.
- **Selling an enterprise layer while keeping the library permissive.** Examples: Probabl (certification, skore, support), Posit (Workbench, Connect, AI), Plotly (Dash Enterprise), Streamlit (inside Snowflake).
- **Moving where the GPUs are.** marimo, Gradio and Coiled's GPU pivot.

# Failure patterns
- **Product pivots without a stable OSS anchor.** Observable went through Framework, then Canvases, then Notebooks 2.0, lost momentum and has raised no new funding since 2021.
- **Over-claiming in open-core launches.** Deepnote's "Jupyter successor" message cost it goodwill at launch.
- **Unfunded volunteer infrastructure.** conda-forge's token leak, CRAN's tiny team and QGIS's budget gap show how thin the margins are.
- **Non-profits budgeting to aspirational revenue.** NumFOCUS's expenses doubled between 2022 and 2024, then it had to cut staff[^propublica-numfocus][^numfocus-new-structure].
- **Single-funder dependency.** Projects that relied on EOSS or a single NSF award had nothing to fall back on in 2025.
- **Abrupt monetization breaks trust.** Anaconda's CI-breaking ToS prompt is the clearest case[^gh-runner-issue].
