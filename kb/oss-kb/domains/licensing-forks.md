# Executive summary
- **Foundation forks with backing won the community fight.** [Valkey](/projects/licensing-forks/valkey.md) (LF), [OpenTofu](/projects/licensing-forks/opentofu.md) (CNCF), [OpenSearch](/projects/licensing-forks/opensearch.md) (OpenSearch Software Foundation) and [OpenBao](/projects/licensing-forks/openbao.md) (OpenSSF) all grew through Oct 2026. None declined the way forks usually do. Valkey kept higher commit velocity than Redis even after Redis went back to AGPL.[^redmonk-valkey][^percona-erosion]
- **Relicensing vendors mostly did fine financially.** Redis Ltd passed $300M ARR (Jan 2026).[^redis-300m] Elastic grew 17% in FY26 and its stock jumped ~23% on Q1 FY27.[^elastic-fy26-q4][^elastic-q1fy27] HashiCorp sold to IBM for $6.4B.[^ibm-closes] What they lost was control of the open ecosystem, not revenue.
- **Reversals and abandonments both happened.** Elastic (Aug 2024) and Redis (May 2025) went back to AGPL.[^redis-agpl-blog] Over the same period MinIO archived its AGPL project (Apr 2026), and CockroachDB took development fully private (Sept 2026), citing AI.[^minio-gh][^crdb-private]
- **Vendors restricted distribution instead of licenses.** Broadcom/Bitnami, Perforce/Puppet and MinIO left the code licenses alone but removed free binaries, images, consoles or public repos.[^bitnami-issue][^minio-gh]
- **Trademark and infrastructure control decided governance fights.** The CNCF kept NATS by holding its trademarks.[^cncf-nats-agree] WordPress.org's control of distribution was the weapon in the WP Engine fight, which led to an antitrust case whose claims were revived on Sept 24, 2026, when the court also held that the WordPress Foundation, not Automattic, owns the WordPress marks. Days earlier a board coup at Automattic had failed (Sept 2026).[^sej-antitrust][^wht-tm-ruling][^tc-leave] Rust took the opposite path, finalising a relaxed, community-friendly trademark policy in Jan 2025.[^rf-tm]
- **"Fair Source" stalled.** It peaked at 13 listed adopters, the last being Liquibase in Sept 2025.[^fair-companies]
- **AI is the new pressure on licenses.** AI clean-room rewrites (chardet LGPL→MIT), AI-found vulnerabilities, and AI as the stated reason for CockroachDB going closed all challenge the assumption that copyleft protects either commons or businesses.[^chardet-willison][^crdb-private][^valkey-security-2026]

# Did relicensing work?
A four-part scorecard of the defensive relicensings visible in this period:

| Case | Move | Community outcome | Business outcome | Did it "work"? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Redis (2024 SSPL/RSAL → 2025 AGPL) | Defensive relicense, then reversal | Lost: Valkey became the default for clouds and distros, and ~37.5% of pre-fork contributors left[^percona-erosion] | ARR >$300M (Jan 2026), but layoffs in July 2026[^redis-300m][^globes-layoffs] | **Partly.** Revenue grew, but the reason given (stopping cloud free-riding) failed. AWS/Google now run Valkey and pay Redis nothing |
| Elastic (2021 SSPL → 2024 +AGPL) | Defensive relicense, then reversal | Permanent split: OpenSearch keeps growing[^lf-opensearch-members] | FY26 +17%, profitable; stock up on Q1 FY27[^elastic-fy26-q4][^elastic-q1fy27] | **Yes commercially, no for the community.** The AGPL return had no visible revenue cost |
| HashiCorp (2023 BSL) | Defensive relicense, then sale | Lost: OpenTofu and OpenBao lead the open ecosystem[^infoq-tf115][^techtarget-openbao] | $6.4B IBM exit, despite a $190.7M GAAP net loss in fiscal 2024 (FY ended Jan 2024)[^ibm-closes][^hcp-fy24] | **Yes for shareholders.** For users it brought IBM-style pruning (CDKTF)[^cdktf-docs] |
| CockroachDB (BSL → Enterprise → private) | Escalating restriction | No fork (nothing recent was open to fork) | Not disclosed[^crdb-license][^crdb-private] | **Unknown.** The project left open source entirely |
| Grafana (2021 AGPL) | Copyleft, not source-available | No fork | >$6B (2024) → ~$9B reported (2026); $600M+ ARR by Aug 2026[^grafana-tc][^info-9b][^grafana-600m] | **Yes, best result in the sample** |
| Liquibase (2025 FSL) | Fair Source | Credibility hit, no fork | Not disclosed[^liquibase-fsl] | **Too early** |
| Linkerd/Buoyant (2024 stable-build paywall) | Kept Apache-2.0; charged for stable builds | No fork | Buoyant said it became profitable within ~8 months[^tt-linkerd] | **Yes, per the vendor.** No independent figures |
| Synadia/NATS (2025 attempted BSL) | Attempted clawback | Foundation kept the project[^cncf-nats-agree] | Synadia kept commercial freedom only | **No.** The attempt failed in a week |

**Reading the evidence:**
1. **No relicensing visibly hurt revenue.** Revenue and exits mostly grew afterwards. The available data does not support a strong causal claim either way.
2. **Every relicensing that targeted hyperscalers produced a durable, hyperscaler-funded foundation fork.** The license did not stop AWS or Google. It moved them to the fork, which is the opposite of the stated goal.
3. **Copyleft (AGPL) has done better than source-available licensing** on both measures. Grafana never got forked, and Elastic and Redis both ended up back on AGPL. AI-assisted reimplementation (chardet) now raises doubts about copyleft too.[^chardet-willison]
4. **The real defence is the managed service and enterprise sales,** not the license. Every growing vendor here cites cloud and AI products (vector search, agent memory) as its growth engine.[^redis-300m][^elastic-q1fy27]

# Trends
1. **Forks are winning, and they are funded forks.** Valkey, OpenTofu, OpenSearch and OpenBao all have corporate backers whose business models the relicense threatened (hyperscalers, TACOS vendors, sovereign clouds). See [Valkey](/projects/licensing-forks/valkey.md) and [OpenTofu](/projects/licensing-forks/opentofu.md).[^redmonk-valkey]
2. **SSPL is going out of use, and AGPL is the place vendors settle.** Elastic and Redis both moved to tri-licenses that include AGPL, and Grafana's early AGPL choice looks best in hindsight.[^redis-agpl-blog][^grafana-tc]
3. **Vendors now restrict distribution instead of changing licenses.** Bitnami images, MinIO binaries and console, Puppet packages and Mattermost history limits all stayed open-licensed on paper while removing what users actually depended on.[^bitnami-issue][^minio-gh]
4. **Trademark and infrastructure decide governance.** NATS stayed open because the CNCF held the marks. WordPress.org's plugin directory and trademark were the leverage against WP Engine, and Deno is still fighting Oracle over the "JavaScript" trademark.[^cncf-nats-agree][^sej-antitrust] See [Deno v. Oracle](/events/2024-11-deno-petitions-cancel-javascript-trademark.md).
5. **AI is breaking assumptions behind licenses.** AI clean-room rewrites (chardet), AI-found CVE surges (Valkey), AI as the reason for closing source (CockroachDB), and contribution lockdowns over AI-generated PRs (Ladybird).[^chardet-willison][^valkey-security-2026][^crdb-private][^xda-ladybird] Open-weight model licensing is moving in the same restrictive direction (see [Kimi K3 / flagship license tightening](/events/2026-07-kimi-k3-and-flagship-license-tightening.md)).
6. **Sovereignty is a new source of demand for neutral projects.** The CRA's steward regime, the Dutch NixOS desktop and OpenBao in sovereign clouds all favour foundation-governed or community projects over single-vendor open core.[^heise-cra-oss][^reg-dawo][^techtarget-openbao] See [CRA reporting obligations](/events/2026-09-cra-reporting-obligations-start.md).
7. **Governance institutions are fragile.** The OSI paused its elections (then appointed a permanent executive director in April 2026), NixOS governance bodies are dissolving, and Automattic's board was purged.[^osi-2026-elections][^osi-obrien][^linuxiac-coreteam][^tc-leave]
8. **Acquirers absorb relicensed companies and cut adjacent projects.** IBM (HashiCorp, then Confluent), Qualcomm (Arduino) and Broadcom (Bitnami).[^ibm-closes][^cdktf-docs]
9. **A no-relicense middle path exists.** Linkerd kept Apache-2.0 but charged for stable builds, and Buoyant reports profitability. It is the distribution-restriction pattern done openly and with a small-company free tier.[^tt-linkerd]

# Success patterns
- **A neutral home plus a permissive license plus funded maintainers** (Valkey, OpenTofu). Moving the fork into a foundation quickly makes it the safe default for distros and clouds.
- **Shipping what upstream withheld:** OpenTofu's state encryption and provider iteration, and OpenBao's free namespaces.
- **Copyleft early, before a hyperscaler competitor exists** (Grafana).
- **Revenue from managed services and AI features rather than license enforcement** (Elastic, Redis).
- **Foundation trademark ownership** as a structural safeguard (NATS).

# Failure patterns
- **Relicensing after the hyperscaler is already a competitor.** It causes a fork the vendor cannot later undo (Redis).
- **Cutting existing free users' functionality or binaries without warning,** especially security fixes (MinIO's CVE-2025-62506 images, Bitnami brownouts).
- **One person controlling a project's distribution infrastructure** (WordPress.org).
- **Calling non-OSI licenses "open source"** (Liquibase).
- **Elected governance without clear delegation** (NixOS) and **opaque elections** (OSI).

# Open questions / watchlist for next 6 months
- **WP Engine v. Automattic** after the Sept 24, 2026 ruling: the Oct 7 sanctions hearing over missing evidence, summary judgment (late 2026/early 2027) and the Oct 19, 2027 trial; whether the WordPress Foundation's ownership of the marks changes Automattic's licensing strategy. Watch WordPress market share and FAIR adoption.
- **Does CockroachDB's "AI made us close source" rationale spread** to other BSL/source-available vendors?
- **Valkey vs Redis feature divergence** (search in Valkey core, Redis Query Engine). Does any distro or cloud switch back to Redis under AGPL?
- **OpenTofu CNCF incubation, and IBM's next moves on Terraform and Vault** licensing and pricing.
- **OSI governance redesign** under new executive director Duane O'Brien (since Apr 2026), and whether board elections resume.
- **CRA steward obligations** ahead of Dec 2027. Will foundations formally register as stewards?
- **chardet-style AI relicensing:** a first legal test case would change how much copyleft protects.
- **Deno v. Oracle** TTAB case: trial briefing was scheduled into 2026, but no public update has appeared since June 2025 and no decision was found.
