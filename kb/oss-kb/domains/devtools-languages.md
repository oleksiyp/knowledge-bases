# Executive summary
- **AI labs and cloud platforms bought the developer-tools startups.** In seven months the three most important VC-backed JS/Python tooling companies were all acquired. Anthropic bought Bun on 2025-12-02, and Bun had zero revenue at the time. OpenAI agreed to buy Astral (uv/Ruff/ty) on 2026-03-19. Cloudflare bought VoidZero (Vite/Vitest/Rolldown/Oxc) on 2026-06-04.[^bun-joins-anthropic][^astral-openai][^cf-voidzero] The fourth such company, Deno, went the other way: it laid off staff in March 2026 and shut down Deploy Classic.[^bushell-deno]
- **Developer tooling moved to native code, much of it Rust.** TypeScript 7.0 shipped on a Go compiler (2026-07-08, 8–12x faster). Vite 8 runs on Rolldown, and Bun merged a Zig-to-Rust rewrite of more than 1M lines, generated by AI, on 2026-05-14.[^wiki-ts][^reg-bun-rust] Rust entered the TIOBE top 10 in July 2026 and lost its "experimental" label in the Linux kernel (Dec 2025).[^wiki-rust][^lwn-rust-exp]
- **AI coding agents now shape the toolchain.** Single-binary runtimes and fast package managers matter because agents run them in tight loops. AI-written code is now a fault line in governance: Zig and Codeberg ban it, while Bun and Ladybird embrace it (Ladybird even closed outside PRs because AI made them untrustworthy).[^zig-codeberg][^wiki-codeberg][^wiki-ladybird][^ladybird-feb26]
- **More projects moved under foundations and nonprofits.** React moved to a React Foundation at the Linux Foundation (announced 2025-10-07, launched 2026-02-24). Ghostty became a nonprofit under Hack Club's fiscal sponsorship. Node.js cut back to one major release a year.[^react-foundation][^ghostty-nonprofit][^node-schedule]
- **Security failures kept recurring.** Next.js had a middleware auth bypass (CVE-2025-29927). React2Shell, a CVSS 10 RCE in React Server Components, was exploited within hours. npm saw the Shai-Hulud worm compromise more than 500 packages followed by successor waves into August 2026, Open VSX was hit repeatedly by GlassWorm, and Next.js shipped two more critical fixes in August 2026.[^vercel-postmortem][^wiki-next][^cisa-kev][^cisa-npm][^wiki-npm][^thn-glassworm-72][^next-blog]
- **Charging for developer tools stayed hard.** VoidZero dropped its paid Vite+ license and Astral shut down pyx. The companies that did make money sold platforms, not tools: Vercel reached a $9.3B valuation and Zed sells AI usage and business plans.[^viteplus-alpha][^wiki-vercel][^zed-blog]
- **Language releases were strong and mostly about performance.** Python 3.14 made free-threading officially supported. Mojo reached 1.0 and open-sourced its compiler weeks after Qualcomm closed its ~$3.1B purchase of Modular. Swift 6.3 shipped the first official Android SDK. Zig reached 0.17.[^whatsnew-314][^wiki-mojo][^modular-qcom-close][^swift-63][^zig-017]

# Trends
1. **AI labs and cloud platforms buying developer tools.** Bun went to Anthropic, Astral to OpenAI and VoidZero to Cloudflare. Each stayed permissively licensed, each came with a "nothing changes" pledge, and none had proven revenue. See [Anthropic acquires Bun](/events/2025-12-anthropic-acquires-bun.md), [OpenAI acquires Astral](/events/2026-03-openai-acquires-astral.md) and [Cloudflare acquires VoidZero](/events/2026-06-cloudflare-acquires-voidzero.md).
2. **Native rewrites, increasingly AI-generated.** Examples are TS-Go, Rolldown/Oxc, Biome, Rspack, Bun's Rust port and Ladybird's Rust JS engine. Rewrites are now cheap; the scarce resources are review capacity and trust. See [Bun's Rust rewrite](/events/2026-05-bun-rust-rewrite.md) and [TypeScript 7](/events/2026-07-typescript-7-native-go.md).
3. **Policy on AI-written code as a governance fault line.** Zig and Codeberg ban it, Ladybird closed outside PRs, and Gram forked Zed. On the other side, Bun and Ladybird use AI to do the porting. See [Zig moves to Codeberg](/events/2025-11-zig-moves-to-codeberg.md).
4. **Foundations and nonprofits as continuity guarantees.** Examples are the React Foundation, Ghostty under Hack Club, the ZSF, Codeberg e.V., the Ladybird Browser Initiative, and Servo at LF Europe. See [React Foundation](/events/2025-10-react-foundation-announced.md).
5. **Registries are a security choke point.** npm (Shai-Hulud), Open VSX (GlassWorm) and PyPI (the NSF-grant fight) all show it. Fixes include short-lived tokens, trusted publishing, paid managed registries and AI-company donations. See [Shai-Hulud](/events/2025-09-shai-hulud-npm-worm.md) and [PSF NSF grant](/events/2025-10-psf-withdraws-nsf-grant.md).
6. **Framework security tied to one vendor.** CVE-2025-29927 hit only self-hosted Next.js, and React2Shell came from a protocol Vercel helped shape. See [Next.js middleware bypass](/events/2025-03-nextjs-middleware-auth-bypass.md) and [React2Shell](/events/2025-12-react2shell-rsc-rce.md).
7. **Trust in GitHub declining.** The CEO left (Aug 2025), Copilot became pervasive, and Actions reliability complaints grew. The result was the first meaningful move to Codeberg/Forgejo.
8. **Fewer business models that work.** Commercial licenses for tools were reversed (Vite+), registries were shut (pyx), and runtime cloud offerings shrank (Deno Deploy). What worked was hosting (Vercel), AI usage billing (Zed), or being acquired.

# Success patterns
- **Speed users notice right away**: uv, Ruff, Vite, TS 7 and Bun all won on order-of-magnitude performance.
- **Compatibility with the incumbent**: uv speaks pip/PyPI, Rspack speaks webpack, Bun and Deno added Node/npm compatibility, and Node absorbed TypeScript.
- **Permissive licenses plus a strategic acquirer**: MIT/Apache tools sitting inside agent loops became acquisition targets, and permissive licensing made the communities comfortable with the deals.
- **Neutral or non-commercial stewardship**: Neovim, Godot, Ghostty, Homebrew and Python keep developers' trust without VC.

# Failure patterns
- **Owning a registry or cloud without enough adoption**: Deno's JSR and Deploy, and Astral's pyx.
- **Tiered licensing of beloved OSS**: Vite+ commercial tiers were withdrawn before launch.
- **Security debt in central infrastructure**: npm tokens and install scripts, Open VSX publisher verification, and the RSC serialization protocol.
- **Brand churn**: Remix → React Router v7 → React-free Remix 3.
- **Losing a flagship adopter over policy**: Zig's no-AI stance versus Bun's AI-first workflow.

- Do uv, Ruff, ty, Bun and Vite stay neutral under OpenAI, Anthropic and Cloudflare? Watch roadmap priorities, release cadence, and any license or telemetry changes.

### Trend notes (pass 2)
- **Frameworks have become cloud distribution channels.** Vercel owns the Next.js, Nuxt and Turborepo teams and employs Svelte's creator. Cloudflare owns Astro and VoidZero. So far, "same MIT license, same governance" pledges have held.
- **Hosting beats feature-gating.** Hosted add-ons sold on top of MIT frameworks failed: Astro Studio, NuxtLabs add-ons and CrabNebula Cloud. Gating core features caused backlash (Nx Powerpack cache). The winners sell compute or hosting around an unchanged core: Laravel Cloud, Expo EAS and prefix.dev channels.
- **Stewardship crises in older ecosystems.** Ruby Central's seizure of RubyGems produced a fork (gem.coop), a transfer to Ruby core and the steward's own financial crisis within seven months. The PHP Foundation delivers well but faces donor fatigue. Bazel and Flutter answered single-vendor fragility with shared stewardship (the BUILD Foundation, Canonical) rather than forks; the Flock fork fizzled.
- **AI agents reopened version control and strained GitHub.** jj, GitButler, ERSC and Entire all pitch "version control for agents." Git Merge 2026 was hosted by GitButler and OpenAI, not GitHub. Meanwhile GitHub, now part of Microsoft CoreAI, suffered AI-traffic outages, AI-slop PR floods and a breach.
- **Developer machines are the new supply-chain target.** Nx shows the escalation: CI injection → npm malware that drives local AI CLIs (Aug 2025) → a poisoned editor extension that breached GitHub itself (May 2026).
- **Typed and compiled languages are winning in the agent era.** Gradual types reached Elixir, and Gleam grew. Go and Kotlin thrived. Even DHH moved HEY's backend from Ruby to Rust.
- **Solo-maintainer infrastructure is finding new patrons.** mise is funded by an AI startup (Entire) and a Linux distro foundation (Omacom). Gleam and Bevy run on sponsors, and Bevy says it is "drastically underfunded."
