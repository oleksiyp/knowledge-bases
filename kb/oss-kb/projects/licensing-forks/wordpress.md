# Summary
WordPress is the period's main governance failure where licensing was not the problem: the code stayed GPL. The fight was over who controls the commons, meaning the WordPress.org plugin directory, update servers and the trademark. Starting in September 2024 Matt Mullenweg, who controls both Automattic and WordPress.org, attacked WP Engine and demanded 8% of its revenue as a trademark royalty. WordPress.org cut off WP Engine's access (Sept 25), WP Engine sued (Oct 2), WordPress.org took over the ACF plugin as "Secure Custom Fields" (Oct 12), and a federal judge issued a preliminary injunction against Automattic on Dec 10, 2024.[^tc-explainer][^tc-lawsuit][^wporg-scf][^lwn-injunction] On Sept 24, 2026 the judge revived WP Engine's four antitrust counts and ruled that Automattic cannot press trademark claims itself, because the WordPress Foundation, not Automattic, owns the marks. A jury trial is set for Oct 19, 2027.[^wht-tm-ruling][^thebuild-oct] The fallout included Automattic layoffs (16%, April 2025), the Linux Foundation's decentralised FAIR package manager (June 2025), a slipping market share (43.6% → 41.9% between June 2025 and May 2026), a run of core RCE vulnerabilities in 2026, and a failed board attempt in September 2026 to put Mullenweg on leave.[^tc-layoffs][^lf-fair][^reg-share][^tc-leave][^tc-return] Verdict: OSS in crisis, business struggling.

# OSS successes
- Releases continue: 7.0 (May 2026) added AI foundations and a modernised admin.[^tt-wp70]
- The community created structural hedges against single-person control, most visibly FAIR under the Linux Foundation.[^lf-fair]

# OSS failures / risks
- Infrastructure that looked neutral (WordPress.org) turned out to be one person's leverage. The ACF takeover showed that the plugin directory can be used against a competitor.[^wporg-scf][^acf-response]
- Market share is declining. The Register notes the correlation but says there is no causal evidence.[^reg-share]
- 2026 brought several critical core vulnerabilities with fast exploitation.[^bleeping-wp2shell][^helpnet-cve]

# Business successes
- n/a for WordPress itself. WP Engine kept operating under the injunction.[^lwn-injunction]

# Business failures / risks
- Automattic: layoffs, a board crisis, and executives leaving (CFO/interim CEO and general counsel asked to step down).[^tc-layoffs][^techzine-board]
- Litigation risk is growing. All four antitrust claims against Automattic were revived on Sept 24, 2026, and Automattic lost standing to assert the WordPress trademarks itself (other counterclaims survive). A sanctions hearing over allegedly destroyed messages was set for Oct 7, 2026.[^wht-tm-ruling][^sej-antitrust][^thebuild-oct]
- Corrected in pass 2: "Automattic's trademark counterclaims also proceed" → the court held Automattic is neither owner nor exclusive licensee of the marks, so its own trademark claims cannot proceed.[^wht-tm-ruling]

# Lessons
- A copyleft license does not protect a project from governance risk if distribution infrastructure and trademarks belong to one person.
- Trademark is the new lever in open-source fights. It appears in WordPress, NATS, JavaScript and Rust.
- Communities respond by decentralising distribution (FAIR) rather than forking the code.
