# Summary
Mira Murati's Thinking Machines Lab (founded early 2025; ~$2B seed at $12B led by a16z, Jul 2025) first shipped Tinker (Oct 2025), an API for fine-tuning open-weight models[^wiki-tml][^tc-40b]. On 15–16 Jul 2026 it released Inkling — a 975B-parameter MoE (~41B active, 1M context, multimodal input) trained from scratch on ~45T tokens under Apache-2.0, with an architecture modelled on DeepSeek-V3 and SFT data generated by Kimi K2.5[^ghacks-inkling][^winbuzzer-inkling] — followed by Inkling-Small (276B/12B active) on 30 Jul 2026[^tml-inkling-small]. It shows the inversion of 2023: well-funded US labs now build on Chinese open models. The business is funded richly (Nvidia gigawatt deal; $1B raise at $40B under discussion in Sept 2026) but has lost four co-founders to OpenAI[^cnbc-nvidia][^tc-40b][^fortune-defections][^tnw-weng].

# OSS successes
- Apache-2.0 at near-1T scale from a US lab, with day-one availability on major inference clouds[^ghacks-inkling].
- Smaller Inkling-Small makes the family usable on far less hardware[^tml-inkling-small].

# Lessons
- Open weights let new labs skip pretraining-architecture R&D — Chinese open releases became the West's starting point.
- An open-weight flagship can serve as the funnel for a paid fine-tuning platform (Tinker) rather than for API sales.
