# Summary

The two-year window was the most active period of COSS M&A on record. Three public COSS companies disappeared from the market — **HashiCorp** (IBM, $6.4B EV, closed 2025-02-27)[^tc-ibm-hashicorp], **Couchbase** (Haveli, ~$1.5B, closed 2025-09-24)[^couchbase-close] and **Confluent** (IBM, ~$11B, $31/share, closed 2026-03-17)[^ibm-confluent-close] — and the largest-ever COSS acquisition was announced in W3: **Nvidia–Hugging Face for ~$12.9B** ($11.9B plus up to $1B in retention awards; 2026-09-03; expected to close H1 2027)[^nvidia-hf][^cnbc-nvidia-hf]. Beneath the headline deals, three patterns dominate: (1) a **Postgres land-grab** by data platforms (Databricks–Neon ~$1B, Snowflake–Crunchy ~$250M, Supabase–Turso)[^yf-neon][^cnbc-crunchy][^turso-supabase]; (2) **AI labs buying the OSS dev tools their agents run on** (Anthropic–Bun, OpenAI–Astral)[^bun-anthropic][^astral-openai]; (3) **strategic rescues of OSS projects whose monetization AI broke** (Shopify–Tailwind Labs)[^tailwind-shopify]. LF data shows COSS M&A medians at $482M vs $34M for closed-source peers (14x)[^lf-coss-2025].

# Analysis

- **IBM is the consolidator of last resort for public COSS**: Red Hat (2019), HashiCorp (2025), Confluent (2026). See [IBM's open source portfolio](/projects/coss-market/ibm-open-source-portfolio.md).
- **Mid-cap public COSS companies trading at modest multiples became targets** (HashiCorp, Couchbase, Confluent); GitLab has been the subject of recurring sale talk since 2024 (see [public companies](/projects/coss-market/coss-ipos-and-public-companies.md)).
- **AI labs and AI-coding companies became acquirers of OSS developer tools.** Unlike classic acqui-hires, the projects were kept MIT/Apache and are maintained in public (Bun, Astral pledged this explicitly)[^bun-anthropic][^astral-openai] — but strategic control moved into the AI-lab perimeter.
- **Neutral hubs are now strategic assets**: Nvidia agreed to pay ~$12.9B for Hugging Face while pledging that "NVIDIA compute will not be required" to use the hub[^nvidia-hf].

# Lessons
- In a market where AI-adjacent private COSS companies get 10x+ revenue multiples, slower-growing public COSS firms are cheaper to buy than to build against — expect more take-outs.
- Owning the runtime/toolchain that coding agents execute on is now worth paying for, even with $0 revenue (Bun).
- License pledges ("stays MIT") are now standard deal language; governance (who decides the roadmap) is the real change.
