# Projects by domain

* [ai-agents](ai-agents/) - 38 concepts. Two-year review (Oct 2024 – Oct 2026) of open-source AI agent frameworks, coding agents, low-code builders and agent protocols: protocols consolidated under the Linux Foundation's AAIF, terminal coding agents and OpenClaw exploded, low-code builders and IDE forks died, and fair-code n8n became the business winner.
* [ai-apps](ai-apps/) - 35 concepts. Two-year review (Oct 2024 – Oct 2026) of open-source end-user AI apps: chat UIs, local desktop apps, image/video/audio studios, RAG/search/notebook apps, LLMOps and UI frameworks. Adoption exploded, but licences drifted toward source-available, early pioneers went silent, and the winning businesses were cloud add-ons or acquisitions.
* [ai-inference](ai-inference/) - 27 concepts. Oct 2024 – Oct 2026: vLLM and SGLang won the serving war and became VC-backed companies; local AI consolidated around llama.cpp (now under Hugging Face, itself being bought by NVIDIA) and Ollama; foundations absorbed key projects while their commercial stewards were acquired by compute owners (Qualcomm–Modular, Nscale–Anyscale, Cloudflare–Replicate).
* [ai-models](ai-models/) - 18 concepts. Oct 2024 – Oct 2026: Chinese labs (Qwen, DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM, MiniMax) took over open-model adoption and went public or raised at $20-50B+; Meta retreated from open frontier weights; US answered with gpt-oss, Gemma 4 (Apache-2.0), Nemotron and new labs; 2026 flagships began carrying revenue-gated licenses.
* [cloud-native](cloud-native/) - 30 concepts. Oct 2024 – Oct 2026: Kubernetes entered a stable 'boring' era. OpenTelemetry graduated and reshaped observability. Maintainer-starved components (ingress-nginx) were retired, and value consolidated into a few winners: Temporal ($12.55B), Grafana Labs ($600M+ ARR; ~$9B valuation reported, not confirmed), Chronosphere ($3.35B to Palo Alto Networks); small vendors exited (Sidero Labs to Yardi).
* [coss-market](coss-market/) - 12 concepts. The COSS business landscape Oct 2024 to Oct 2026: record AI-driven funding concentrated in a few data/infra platforms, the largest wave of COSS M&A ever (IBM–HashiCorp, IBM–Confluent, Nvidia–Hugging Face), a shrinking public cohort with no US COSS IPO, AI labs buying OSS dev tools, and AI breaking docs-funnel monetization.
* [data-engineering](data-engineering/) - 34 concepts. Oct 2024 – Oct 2026: Iceberg won the format war and the fight moved to catalogs; Kafka modernized while Confluent sold to IBM; Fivetran swallowed dbt Labs, SQLMesh and Great Expectations; Prefect bought Dagster; Databricks hit $190B; dbt reversed its ELv2 Fusion license.
* [databases](databases/) - 32 concepts. Postgres won and the AI-agent 'database per agent' land grab produced $1B+ acquisitions and a $10.5B Supabase. Meanwhile MySQL entered a governance crisis, CockroachDB went fully closed, and standalone vector databases commoditised.
* [devtools-languages](devtools-languages/) - 53 concepts. Oct 2024 to Oct 2026 in runtimes, languages, JS/Python tooling, editors, forges and frameworks. Developer-tool startups were bought by AI labs and cloud platforms (Bun to Anthropic, Astral to OpenAI, VoidZero to Cloudflare), Rust rewrites ran through the toolchain, foundations took on more stewardship, and supply-chain and framework security failures recurred.
* [end-user-apps](end-user-apps/) - 34 concepts. Two-year review (Oct 2024 – Oct 2026) of open-source end-user software: the Linux desktop's gaming-driven surge, Europe's sovereignty-driven migrations, the self-hosting boom (Immich, Home Assistant, Nextcloud), browser economics after the Google remedy, and a wave of governance crises, forks and open-core tightening.
* [hardware-embedded](hardware-embedded/) - 28 concepts. Open source at the hardware layer, covering RISC-V, open silicon and EDA, RTOS and firmware, maker boards, networking firmware, robotics, drones, automotive and 3D printing. Open standards and foundation-hosted firmware thrived. Open-hardware businesses split between scale winners (Raspberry Pi, Espressif, Auterion) and casualties of capital intensity, Chinese competition and the AI-driven DRAM crunch (Efabless, K-Scale, Pine64, Prusa's open-hardware retreat). Chip vendors (Qualcomm, NVIDIA, Google) bought or open-sourced the 'physical AI' stack.
* [licensing-forks](licensing-forks/) - 22 concepts. How license changes, relicensing reversals, community forks and trademark/governance fights played out between Oct 2024 and Oct 2026. Foundation-backed forks (Valkey, OpenTofu, OpenSearch, OpenBao) won the community. Relicensing vendors still mostly grew or exited well. The next stage is control of distribution, closed development and AI-era threats to copyleft.
* [scientific-computing](scientific-computing/) - 32 concepts. Oct 2024 – Oct 2026: the community-run core (NumPy, SciPy, scikit-learn, Matplotlib) stayed healthy on fixed release trains; notebooks fragmented as marimo broke out and was bought by CoreWeave; Anaconda monetized hard (ToS enforcement, $1.5B Series C, M&A spree) while conda-forge and prefix.dev's Rust tooling took the community; US federal grant cuts, the end of CZI's EOSS and NumFOCUS deficits strained the funding base.
* [security-sustainability](security-sustainability/) - 20 concepts. Supply-chain attacks, maintainer burnout, funding, foundations, regulation and AI's impact on open source maintainers, Oct 2024 - Oct 2026: worms and CI-trust attacks dominate, AI floods maintainers with findings, and money finally starts flowing — mostly through foundations and from AI labs.
* [web-platforms](web-platforms/) - 32 concepts. Two-year review (Oct 2024 – Oct 2026) of open-source web platforms: platform companies buying OSS building blocks (Figma–Payload, Vercel–Better Auth), a second wave of source-available relicensing (NocoDB SUL, Directus MSCL, Medusa EE), bootstrapped winners (Ghost, Plausible, Bruno), steward funding stress (Drupal Association, Hasura's pivot) and AI app generation squeezing low-code tools.
