# Meaning

An octopus organization distributes decision-making across teams while maintaining coordination. The term appears in Werner and Le-Brun's continuous-transformation work and in Wunker and Brill's AI-oriented leadership work.[^book][^leadership]

# Two formulations

| Formulation | Authors | Entry point |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous transformation | Jana Werner and Phil Le-Brun | [Framework](/frameworks/continuous-transformation.md) |
| AI-enabled organization | Jonathan Brill and Stephen Wunker | [Framework](/frameworks/ai-enabled-organization.md) |

These are distinct publications. This bundle does not establish who originated the metaphor or whether either formulation derives from the other.

# Interpretation

The useful question is which decisions a team can actually make. An organization chart with many departments does not, by itself, demonstrate distributed authority. The animal analogy should not be interpreted as a requirement to create eight teams.
