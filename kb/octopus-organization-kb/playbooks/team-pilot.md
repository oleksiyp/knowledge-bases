# Scope

This is an illustrative exercise created for this knowledge base, not an official implementation method from either author team. It applies [decision rights](/principles/decision-rights.md), [clarity](/principles/strategic-clarity.md), and [coordination](/principles/coordination.md).

# Example

A software team wants to improve onboarding completion. Choose a reversible change, such as help text, with a measurable customer outcome and a named owner.

# Four-week sequence

| Period | Work | Reviewable output |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Observe the current workflow and identify approval delays. | Baseline, customer problem, decision owner, and boundaries. |
| Week 2 | Run one small experiment within the agreed scope. | Experiment record with hypothesis, exposure, and rollback conditions. |
| Week 3 | Review customer outcomes and any coordination failures. | Results, uncertainties, and a decision to stop, revise, or continue. |
| Week 4 | Review whether authority and information were sufficient. | Updated decision record and a justified next step. |

# Evaluation

Record elapsed decision time, time spent waiting for approval, the customer outcome, and unexpected effects. Compare against the baseline, while noting changes in customer mix or workload. A short pilot cannot establish company-wide causality.

# Exit conditions

Stop or revise if boundaries are breached, results cannot be interpreted, or the team lacks the promised authority. Expand only after reviewing customer value and effects on adjacent teams. A stopped experiment can still yield useful learning.
