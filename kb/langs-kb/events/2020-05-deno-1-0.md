# What happened
Deno 1.0 shipped on 2020-05-13, two years after Ryan Dahl's talk on Node.js design regrets.[^deno-v1] Its distinctive bets were a browser-like sandbox where network, filesystem and environment access require explicit `--allow-*` flags; TypeScript as a first-class input with no extra tooling; dependencies imported by URL with no `package.json` or central registry; and web-standard APIs such as `fetch`.[^deno-v1] Deno Land Inc. was incorporated in March 2021 with a $4.9M seed round.[^deno-company]

# Why it matters
Deno 1.0 marks the start of a real competitive market for JS runtimes. Its ideas were influential: web-standard APIs on the server, built-in TypeScript, and an integrated toolchain (fmt, lint, test). But its ecosystem bets did not survive contact with npm. Deno 2 (October 2024) embraced `package.json`, `node_modules` and npm compatibility.[^deno-v2] Node later absorbed native TypeScript execution. The verdict: the ideas spread, but Deno as the vehicle for them stalled.
