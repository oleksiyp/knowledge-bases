# What happened
On 2024-01-25 the WASI Subgroup of the W3C WebAssembly CG voted to launch WASI 0.2, also called Preview 2 or WASIp2. It declared the APIs stable.[^ba-wasi02] WASI was now defined on top of the component model: typed WIT interfaces, resource handles in place of raw integer file descriptors, and composition across languages without shared linear memory. It shipped two "worlds": `wasi-cli`, which roughly corresponds to POSIX, and `wasi-http`, a request/response proxy world.[^ba-wasi02] The announcement said WASI 0.3, with native async `future`/`stream` types, was "at least a year away".[^ba-wasi02] In the end WASI 0.3.0 shipped on 2026-06-11, more than two years later.[^wasi-p3][^ba-wasi03]

# Why it matters
Preview 2 was a deliberate break from the POSIX-like preview 1 that most toolchains and Docker+Wasm used. The break split the ecosystem. Wasmer had already launched its own POSIX-compatible WASIX in May 2023, and the Bytecode Alliance publicly criticised it.[^infoworld-wasix] Language support for components arrived unevenly, with Rust first and others well behind. Supporters see WASI 0.2 as the foundation for portable, capability-secure plugins and serverless functions. Sceptics note that it took five years from 2019 to reach a stable networking story, and that async took two more. This is a central data point for why server-side Wasm grew slowly.
