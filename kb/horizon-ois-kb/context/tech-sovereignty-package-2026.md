# What was adopted

On **3 June 2026** the Commission presented the **European Technological Sovereignty Package**: the Communication on European Tech Sovereignty accompanied by an **EU Open Source Strategy** — **COM(2026) 503 final**, transmitted to the Council as ST 10220/26 on 4 June 2026[^com-2026-503]. The package's four initiatives are the **Chips Act 2.0**, the **Cloud and AI Development Act (CADA)**, the Open Source Strategy and a Strategic Roadmap for Digitalisation and AI in Energy[^ngi-commons-0603][^com-2026-503]. A parallel Commission strategy on open and interoperable digital ecosystems for public administrations (EC OSPO, code.europa.eu, EU Open Source Solutions Catalogue, procurement) was announced the same day[^ec-news-0603].

# The Open Source Strategy in brief

Four objectives[^com-2026-503][^dsm-oss]:
1. leverage open source for **technological sovereignty**;
2. strengthen a **vibrant open-source ecosystem** (start-ups, stewardship, security, skills);
3. **open and interoperable digital ecosystems for public administrations**, incl. EU institutions (procurement, OSPOs);
4. reinforce **digital standards and international outreach** (incl. the EU Tech Business Offer).

Key facts and measures (all from COM(2026) 503 unless noted)[^com-2026-503]:
- Context: Europe has over 3 million open-source contributors; the EU spends **€264 billion a year** mostly on US proprietary IT; EU funding to open source under the current MFF is put at **€800 million** (NGI, OIS, SIMPL, open-source AI such as OpenEuroLLM, cybersecurity, RISC-V) but "lacks strategic focus on long-term sustainability".
- **First measure: "Scale up the Open Internet Stack to provide a shared catalogue, a one-stop shop for open source building blocks and sovereign solutions in Europe."** Footnote: under WP 2026-2027 the Commission "mobilised an investment of 41.3 million EUR through three calls" (Sovereign Solutions, Support for Scale, Web 4.0/OIS applications).
- Partner with Member States via the **EDIC on Digital Commons** (and explore an EDIC on digital education), prioritising cloud infrastructure and digital-workplace tools, targeting **at least 30 million active users by 2030** for open-source collaboration/productivity tools, instant messaging and secure email.
- Support open, **decentralised social media** (the Commission runs a Mastodon instance).
- Open-source stacks around the **EU Digital Identity Wallet** and **European Business Wallet**; stewardship of reference implementations to a European Digital Public Infrastructure Foundation.
- Promote open-source solutions "in relevant calls for proposals across R&I programmes" to help develop a full EU technology stack; the 2026-2027 WP's support for open-source business models "will be expanded under the new programming period".
- Security: voluntary **CRA Article 25 security-attestation** programmes and an EU assessment framework for open source; ENISA-supported list of most exposed dependencies; an **Open Source Maintenance Instrument** "for supporting the maintenance and security upkeep of essential components, creating a European capacity and capability to fork projects"; mirroring/build capabilities for critical dependencies.
- Skills: master programmes on collaborative open-source development.
- Money: "for all measures under the open source strategy, an estimated **EUR 2 billion** will need to be mobilised by the public and private sectors over the next seven years"; the strategy is linked to the 2028-2034 MFF proposal and the **European Competitiveness Fund (ECF)**.
- Definition: "open source" = software under licences complying with the **OSI Open Source Definition** (e.g. GPL, Apache-2.0, MIT, MPL-2.0, EPL-2.0, EUPL).
- Monitoring: annual discussion in the Digital Decade Board; report to Parliament every three years.

The OSI summarised that over a third of the 29-page document concerns open source and read it as NGI being "scaled up under the new name 'Open Internet Stack'", with a commitment to promote open source "as the default approach" in Horizon Europe[^osi-2026]. Osborne Clarke highlights the OIS as a funding programme to reduce vendor lock-in and the planned procurement guidelines[^osborne-2026].

# Implications for OIS applicants and grantees

- OIS is now a named instrument of EU industrial policy, not only a research topic — expect continuity into **FP10/ECF (2028–2034)**, but the Commission's topic briefings say follow-up funding is "Not planned for 2027", i.e. no further dedicated OIS topic in WP 2027[^si-fiches] (see [/rules/practical-tips.md](/rules/practical-tips.md)).
- Proposals and cascade projects that show **catalogue readiness, CRA-compliant maintenance, public-sector uptake, and EUDI/EBW or Digital Commons EDIC alignment** match the strategy's language.
- Cascade operators are retooling: NLnet paused NGI Zero calls on 12 June 2026, citing the transition to OIS within the Tech Sovereignty package[^nlnet-stocktaking].
