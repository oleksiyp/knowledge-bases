# EuroStack in brief

**EuroStack** is a loose set of initiatives by academics, politicians, commentators and industry aiming at an independent European digital infrastructure that limits dependence on foreign (mostly US) technology[^eurostack-wiki].

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Sept 2024 | Initiative launched at a European Parliament conference organised by Cristina Caffarra, Francesca Bria and Meredith Whittaker[^eurostack-wiki] |
| 10 Jan 2025 | Industry "EuroStack" pitch letter (Caffarra and industry experts), signed by over 80 organisations[^eurostack-wiki] |
| Feb 2025 | Report "EuroStack – A European Alternative for Digital Sovereignty" by Francesca Bria, Paul Timmers and others with Bertelsmann Stiftung and CEPS; launched by UCL IIPP on 13 Feb 2025[^ucl-eurostack][^bertelsmann-eurostack]; it calls for mobilising about **€300 billion over ten years**[^eurostack-wiki] |
| Jun 2025 | European Parliament's ITRE committee endorsed the proposal[^eurostack-wiki] |
| 2025 | Naming dispute over "EuroStack" at EUIPO[^eurostack-wiki] |

The report (lead author Francesca Bria, with Paul Timmers and Fausto Gernone, 13 Feb 2025) spans semiconductors, networks and satellites, software, cloud, quantum, IoT and data/AI; it estimates about €300 billion needed by 2035 with an initial €10 billion European technology fund, and recommends a "Buy European Act", a European Sovereign Tech Fund for decentralised open-source products and a "EuroStack Challenge" competition[^bertelsmann-eurostack].

# Relationship to the Open Internet Stack

- **OIS is a Commission funding/catalogue instrument; EuroStack is an advocacy and policy agenda.** The OIS concentrates on open-source software building blocks (network, OS/firmware, developer supply chain, Web 4.0 components) and their deployment[^wp7-2026]; EuroStack argues for a much broader industrial programme.
- In the 3C topic texts the Commission refers to integrating "solutions developed by the **Open Euro Stack**" and making open-source frameworks available to the "Open Euro Stack Support for Scale" — wording used interchangeably with OIS in WP 2026-2027[^wp7-2026].
- The May 2025 report for DG CNECT situates OIS within the same sovereignty debate (cloud market dominated by US hyperscalers, extraterritorial law, the European Parliament's ITRE own-initiative report on technological sovereignty)[^mackintosh-2025].
- The June 2026 Tech Sovereignty Package adopts much of the vocabulary — sovereignty, reducing dependencies "that can be weaponised", a "full EU technology stack" — while keeping openness to the world[^com-2026-503].

# Points of debate

- **Protectionism vs openness**: economists Cecilia Rikap and Aline Blankertz warned against protectionist elements and argued for investing in core technologies (notably cloud) and using public procurement strategically[^eurostack-wiki].
- **Scale**: EuroStack's ~€300bn vision versus €41.3M for OIS topics in WP 2026-2027 and an estimated €2bn to be mobilised for all open-source-strategy measures over seven years[^eurostack-wiki][^com-2026-503].
- **Open source vs "European"**: OIS topics ask for solutions that are both "Open source and made in Europe"[^wp7-2026]; the Open Source Strategy notes that global open-source ecosystems are increasingly shaped by the US and China[^com-2026-503].

# Why it matters for applicants

Sovereignty framing is now standard in Cluster 4 evaluation language. Explicitly positioning a proposal against dependencies (cloud, OS, app stores, forges, messaging) and towards adoption by European public administrations and SMEs mirrors both EuroStack and Commission policy text.
